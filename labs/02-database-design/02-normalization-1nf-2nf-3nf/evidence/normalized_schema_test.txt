=== Anomaly re-tests, against the ORIGINAL Problem 1 scenarios ===

A. Update anomaly (was: updating product_category required touching every
   row that product appeared in, e.g. CEM-001 in both INV-001 and INV-002)
   Test: UPDATE category SET category_discount_pct = 10.00 WHERE
   category_name = 'Cement';
   Result: UPDATE 1 (single row changed). Every product under Cement
   (currently just CEM-001, but would be true for any number of products)
   now reads 10.00 via the product->category join. No risk of two rows
   disagreeing, because there is only one row holding that fact now.

B. Insert anomaly (was: couldn't add a new product without fabricating
   customer/sale data, or blocked entirely under NOT NULL)
   Test: INSERT INTO product (product_code, product_name, category_id,
   unit_price) VALUES ('TOL-006', 'Screwdriver Set', <Tools category id>,
   220.00);
   Result: INSERT 0 1, succeeded immediately. No invoice/invoice_item row
   needed to record that this product exists — product is its own
   independent entity now.

C. Delete anomaly (was: deleting the only line involving a given
   salesperson/customer pair erased that salesperson's and customer's
   only record entirely)
   Test: DELETE FROM invoice_item WHERE invoice_no='INV-002' AND
   product_code='CEM-001'; (the only invoice_item row involving
   salesperson "Boat" and customer "Apex Builders")
   Result: DELETE 1. customer.customer_name='Apex Builders' and
   salesperson.salesperson_name='Boat' both still present afterward —
   deleting the sale fact no longer deletes the master records, because
   they live in their own tables independent of invoice_item.

Conclusion: all 3 anomalies identified against sales_flat in Problem 1 are
confirmed gone in the Problem 2 normalized schema, verified with live
inserts/updates/deletes, not just by inspection of the DDL.
